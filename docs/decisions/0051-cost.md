# 0051. Cost: spend sent by a pipeline, owners by tag or rule, and anomalies a person decides on

- **Decision:** Proposed
- **Implementation:** In progress
- **Where:** `cost` keeps daily spend lines from AWS, GCP, Azure or any CSV, with owners, budgets, a forecast for the month and anomalies (`ghcr.io/openasy/cost`). Rightsizing recommendations are planned.
- **Priority:** Medium
- **Area:** Architecture

## Context

"Why did the bill go up?" is asked every month, and answered late, by someone reading a cloud console. The data is there: AWS Cost Explorer, the GCP billing export and Azure cost exports all give spend by day, account, service and tag. What is missing is one place across clouds, an owner for every line, a warning before the month ends rather than after, and a record of which spikes were expected.

Reading the bills directly means holding billing credentials for every account, which is more trust than a cost view needs.

## Decision

- **A pipeline sends the bill.** Spend lines are one day of one service in one account, in JSON, NDJSON or CSV with tags as columns. The product holds no cloud credentials. A converter for Cost Explorer's answer ships in the repository, with no dependencies.
- **A day sent again replaces what was there**, so a daily job can send the whole month; with `replace`, lines the new export no longer has disappear. Lines with the same key in one call are added together. A wrong line is named and nothing of the call is kept. Amounts are kept as millionths, so sums do not drift.
- **Every line gets an owner** if it can: a `team` tag wins, then the first rule that matches its account, service or another tag. What nobody owns is shown as a share, so tagging has a number to improve.
- **One reporting currency.** Spend in another currency counts once an admin sets its rate, and until then every page says what was left out. Rates are not fetched.
- **The forecast** is the spend so far plus the last seven days' rate for the days left. **Budgets** are monthly, for a team or a tag value, with alerts at 50%, 80% and 100% and a burn rate.
- **An anomaly** is a day of one service in one account above the mean of the 14 days before plus three standard deviations, and by more than a minimum. A person acknowledges it or marks it expected with a reason; the decision stays when the day is sent again.

## Consequences

- The product is small and needs no outbound access. Its data is as fresh as the pipeline that sends it, usually a day behind the cloud.
- The anomaly rule is simple on purpose: it can be explained in one sentence. Seasonal services raise anomalies an analyst will mark expected.
- Rightsizing needs utilisation data from Infrastructure or the cloud, and follows as its own decision.
